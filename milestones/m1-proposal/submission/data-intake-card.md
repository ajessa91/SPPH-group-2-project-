# M1: Data Intake Card

## Dataset Information

**Dataset Name:** Hospital beds (per 10,000 population)

**Source and Steward:** World Health Organization (WHO), Global Health Observatory (GHO).

**Source URL:** https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/indicators/indicator-details/GHO/hospital-beds-%28per-10-000-population%29

**Date Accessed:** September 18, 2026 (original data source review).

**Access Method:** Publicly available through the WHO Global Health Observatory website. We plan to download the indicator data in CSV format.

**Terms of Use:** Publicly accessible WHO data. The original source will be cited, and any applicable WHO reuse terms will be checked before redistribution.

**Geography:** Global, including countries with available observations.

**Time Coverage:** Varies by country and reporting year. WHO indicates that the expected frequency of data dissemination is every 2–3 years.

**File Format:** CSV.

**Data Level:** Aggregated country-level indicator data.

**Unit of Analysis:** One country in one reported year.

## Key Variables

- Country: Identifies the country associated with each observation.
- Year: Indicates the reporting year.
- Hospital beds per 10,000 population: Measures the number of hospital beds available relative to the population.

The exact column names will be confirmed after downloading the CSV.

## Privacy and Security

The dataset contains aggregated country-level statistics rather than individual patient records. There is a low risk of identifying individuals through these data.

The analysis will use publicly available information and will not include private patient records or identifiable health information.

## Stewardship and Equity Considerations

Differences in hospital bed availability should be interpreted carefully. Countries have different healthcare systems, resources, population needs, and reporting practices.

A country with fewer hospital beds should not automatically be described as having a worse healthcare system. Hospital bed availability does not capture staffing, quality of care, geographic accessibility, or the availability of outpatient services.

We will avoid stigmatizing countries and explain the limitations of using this indicator for international comparisons.

## Indigenous Data Sovereignty

The proposed dataset does not specifically identify Indigenous communities, territories, or services. Therefore, the analysis does not currently require community-specific Indigenous data governance procedures.

If the scope changes to include Indigenous populations or services, CARE and OCAP principles will be reviewed before proceeding.

## Known Limitations

- Not every country reports data for the same years.
- Different reporting systems may affect comparability.
- Missing observations may limit analysis.
- The indicator does not measure overall healthcare quality or access.
- WHO compiles information from multiple national and regional sources.

## KT Framing

This indicator can help communicate international differences in inpatient healthcare resources while emphasizing that hospital bed availability alone cannot measure the quality, accessibility, or overall effectiveness of healthcare.
